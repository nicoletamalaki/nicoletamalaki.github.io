01 · The invitation

Welcome to Common Ground

Common Ground is a fictional youth NGO. Your community can pilot one new initiative. All three proposals offer value, but each raises questions about access, participation and care.

Our inquiry lens: Who might be missing from a community decision? Consider opportunity, feasibility, inclusion and trade-offs. There is no predetermined winning initiative.

Proposal 01

💻 Digital Skills Swap

Members share practical digital skills through peer-to-peer sessions. New members may worry they have nothing to teach; devices and access may vary.

Proposal 02

🌙 Culture & Connection Nights

Regular gatherings built around food, art and conversation. Evening schedules and travel may exclude some members.

Proposal 03

💬 Stories & Voices Café

A welcoming space for personal and community stories. Participants need meaningful choice about whether—and how much—to share.

04 · Different evidence

Your Community Council

Work in a council of four or five. The facilitator assigns a packet. Other councils will receive different information. You will exchange it later. Do not open another council’s packet until the Assembly; your facilitator controls when each information drop is opened.

Select your assigned packet to open the first information drop.

Choose your Ambassador

Before deciding what to eliminate, invite a volunteer to serve as Ambassador. They will give a fair hearing to whichever proposal the council considers eliminating—even if it is not their own favourite. No one is required to speak.

A Tribute to the Idea — 60 seconds. “Before this idea leaves our council, what is the strongest fair case for keeping it?” Use only information you have; do not invent absent members’ views. If you lack information about this initiative, name what you would need to know to judge it fairly.
05 · Share what you knew

The Council Assembly

Each council presents its provisional decision and the information that shaped it. Listen for facts, assumptions and perspectives your own council never encountered.

Assembly protocol

Each council has two minutes to explain its choice and reveal one important piece of information from its packet. Other councils may ask a question, not award points. The aim is a fuller picture—not a winning team.

Assembly question: Which tribute was hardest to give, and what information would have helped?

Reveal the other councils' information

Only open these packets after each council has shared its own evidence. The packet contents are fictional scenario material, not real research findings.

Your council may change its mind, keep its original choice, or remain divided. All three are legitimate when the reasoning is transparent.
06 · Reconsider & reflect

The Decision Trail

Your first individual view and the council's evolving reasoning are preserved as separate moments. A council decision does not erase anyone's individual perspective.

The final reflection happens aloud

What perspective made you think more carefully about your initial choice? Did it change, strengthen or qualify your reasoning—and why?

Discuss in your small group. You do not have to disclose personal experiences or change your opinion.

Your notes are stored locally in this browser when storage is available. On shared devices, export what you want to keep, then clear your notes. No automatic collection or transfer occurs.
